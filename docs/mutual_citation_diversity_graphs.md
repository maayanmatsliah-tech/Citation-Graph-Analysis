# Mutual Citations & Field Diversity — Graph Summary

Three views of how reciprocal citation relates to how broadly papers cite across fields.

## 1. Mutual-citation rate by diversity group

![Mutual-citation rate by year, per diversity group](../outputs/mutual_citation_rate/rate_by_diversity.png)

Papers that stay focused on a single field get a much larger share of their citations returned, while papers that cite across many fields rarely get reciprocated — staying narrow buys reciprocity.

## 2. Share of papers with any mutual citation, by diversity group

![Share of papers with any mutual citation, per diversity group](../outputs/mutual_paper_share/share_by_diversity.png)

Papers that cite many fields are the most likely to have at least *one* citation returned — casting a wider net gives more chances at a reciprocal tie, even though only a tiny fraction of their citations come back.

## 3. Mean mutual citations vs. mean field diversity over time (1975–2023)

![Mean mutual citations vs mean diversity count by year](../outputs/mean_mutual_vs_diversity/mean_mutual_and_diversity_pre2024.png)

Over the decades papers have cited an ever-wider range of fields while getting fewer of their citations returned — as science broadened, it became less of a direct back-and-forth conversation.
